Zero-Trust School Data Protection: Keeping Student & Fee Records Safe

A school holds sensitive information: grades, family contacts and fee payments. Zero trust is a simple idea: nobody and nothing gets access just because they are inside the building or on the school network. Every request is checked.

Role-based access

Each person should see only what their job needs. A class teacher enters marks for their own subjects. The bursar sees fees but not disciplinary notes. The head teacher sees summaries across the school. If one account is stolen, the damage is limited to what that role could reach.

Good student record privacy also depends on a clear rule for who may see what, and on a system that enforces it instead of relying on trust.

Encryption

  • In transit: the system runs over HTTPS so data cannot be read on the way from a phone or computer to the server
  • At rest: the database and backups are encrypted so a stolen disk or leaked backup is unreadable
  • Passwords: stored as salted hashes, never as plain text

Habits that matter as much as technology

Give every staff member their own login, remove accounts when people leave, keep an audit trail of who changed a mark or a payment, and take regular backups that you have actually tested restoring.
