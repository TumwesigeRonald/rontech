Best CBC Report Card & Mark Sheet Software for Secondary Schools in Uganda

End of term is the busiest week in a school. Teachers add up marks, work out grades, rank students and write comments, often by hand and often late at night. One wrong total can change a grade and start an argument with a parent.

What the software does for you

A modern school system takes the marks a teacher enters and does the repetitive work automatically:

  • Adds up scores and applies the grading scale without arithmetic mistakes
  • Supports the competency-based reporting used under the NCDC curriculum, including continuous assessment alongside exam scores
  • Ranks classes and works out averages in seconds
  • Prints report cards in one batch, with the school's own layout

Where AI helps

Calculation is rules-based and should stay that way, so it is predictable and checkable. AI is most useful on top of the marks: spotting students whose results have dropped across several subjects, flagging subjects where a whole class is struggling, and drafting a first version of report comments that the teacher then edits.

We build school management systems for secondary schools in Luweero and across Uganda, with report cards for both O-Level and A-Level, continuous assessment tracking and fee records in one place.

Time saved

Schools that move from hand-calculated mark sheets to an automated system typically report saving tens of hours per term across the teaching staff. The exact figure depends on school size and how much was done by hand before, so measure your own baseline first.

Keep a human in the loop

Teachers should always review results before they are released. Automation removes arithmetic errors, but it cannot know that a mark was entered against the wrong student. A short review step catches that.
